Community composition was assessed using Aitchison distance calculated from centered log-ratio (CLR)-transformed data to account for the compositional nature of metabarcoding datasets. Community composition was defined as the overall taxonomic structure of fungal assemblages based on the relative abundances of all ASVs detected in each sample.
The effect of environmental variables (temperature, moisture, pH, soil organic matter, NH4, NO3, and Olsen P) on fungal community composition was tested using PERMANOVA (adonis2), with marginal effects computed to estimate the independent contribution of each variable while controlling for the others. Analyses were restricted to soil samples, where environmental measurements were available.
To visualize environmental gradients associated with community variation, distance-based redundancy analysis (db-RDA; capscale) was performed using the same Aitchison distance matrices. Environmental variables were standardized (z-scores) prior to analysis, and their relationships with fungal community composition were represented as vectors in ordination space.
Permutation tests (999 permutations) were used to assess the significance of both global models and individual environmental variables. Variance inflation factors (VIFs) were calculated to assess multicollinearity among predictors.
